<h2>Do you need antivirus on Windows 11 at all?</h2>
<p>Yes, every Windows 11 PC needs antivirus protection. Yours already has it. Microsoft Defender Antivirus ships with Windows 11 and starts protecting your PC the moment you first switch it on. There is nothing to download, nothing to pay for, and no subscription to renew. It checks every file you open, download, or run, without you doing a thing.</p>
<p>Antivirus is software that spots and stops harmful programs, called malware. Viruses, ransomware, and spyware are all types of malware. Viruses copy themselves into your files. Ransomware locks your files and demands money to release them. Spyware watches what you type and sends it to a stranger. Defender guards against all three, in real time, which means it checks files as you use them, not just when you ask it to.</p>
<p>That changes the question. You are not choosing whether to have antivirus. You are choosing whether to pay for a second one on top of the one you already own. For most home users, the honest answer is no. Spend that money on the things no antivirus can do for you, like backups and strong passwords. This guide shows you how to check Defender is working, what the lab tests say, and the few cases where paying extra makes sense.</p>
<div class="note"><b>Check this:</b> A green tick in Windows Security means you are protected. A yellow warning means open the app and read what it says before you do anything else.</div>

<h2>What Windows 11 already does for you</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse4.mm.bing.net/th?q=Windows+Security+app+virus+protection+settings&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="Windows Security app virus protection settings" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Windows Security is the app that holds all of this together. Look for the shield icon in your taskbar, bottom right, near the clock. Inside it you will find Microsoft Defender Antivirus, the Windows Firewall, and SmartScreen. Windows Firewall filters out unwanted network traffic before it reaches your PC. SmartScreen is a warning system that checks downloads and websites against Microsoft&#x27;s list of known threats before you open them.</p>
<p>Defender has three settings that matter most. Real-time protection scans files as you open them. Cloud-delivered protection asks Microsoft&#x27;s servers about files Defender has not seen before. Tamper protection stops other programs from quietly switching Defender off. All three are on by default on a new Windows 11 PC. Leave them on.</p>
<p>Two more features sit in the same app. Smart App Control blocks apps Microsoft does not trust, and it works on new Windows 11 installs. If your PC got Windows 11 through an update, you cannot switch it on without resetting the PC. Controlled folder access stops unknown programs from changing files in your Documents, Pictures, and Desktop folders. It is the closest thing to a ransomware shield Windows offers for free, and you can add your photo and document folders to its list.</p>
<h2>Check that your built-in protection is actually on</h2>
<p>Yours should be on already, but a PC that came with a trial antivirus may have it switched off. Trials are free samples of a paid antivirus that came pre-installed on a new PC. You do not have to wait for it to end. Uninstall it from Settings &gt; Apps &gt; Installed apps, and Defender takes over. This check takes a minute, and it is the single most useful thing in this guide.</p>
<p>Start with the status check. Then run a quick scan, which looks at the places malware usually hides. Quick scans take a few minutes on most PCs. If it finds nothing, you are done. If it finds something, follow the on-screen steps to remove it, then restart your PC and run the scan again.</p>
<p>Next, check the two extra settings. Controlled folder access is worth switching on if you store photos, tax returns, or work files on this PC. It can block a program you trust. If an app you use every day stops saving files, open the same page and allow that app through. SmartScreen should already be on, but check it while you are here.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Click Start, type Windows Security, then press Enter.</li><li>Select Virus and threat protection. The page should say Microsoft Defender Antivirus is turned on.</li><li>Under Virus and threat protection settings, select Manage settings. Check that Real-time protection, Cloud-delivered protection, and Tamper protection are all set to On.</li><li>Go back and select Quick scan. Wait for it to finish, then read the result.</li><li>For ransomware cover, select Virus and threat protection, then Ransomware protection, and turn on Controlled folder access.</li><li>For download warnings, select App and browser control, then Reputation-based protection settings, and check that Check apps and files is On.</li></ol>
<h2>What the independent lab tests say about Defender</h2>
<p>Independent labs test antivirus products on a fixed schedule and publish their methods. AV-TEST is based in Germany. AV-Comparatives is based in Austria. Both test against real malware samples, not just old ones. Their results are the closest thing this subject has to a fact, and they are worth more than any advert or review. Both labs publish their methods and latest results on their own sites, so you can check the newest round yourself.</p>
<p>In recent rounds, Microsoft Defender Antivirus has earned top marks for protection in both labs&#x27; tests. It sits close to the best paid products, not far behind them. The top paid products typically block one or two more threats out of hundreds than Defender does. Usability scores are strong, with very few false alarms. A false alarm is when an antivirus flags a safe file as dangerous.</p>
<p>Lab scores measure blocking malware samples. They say nothing about scams, which is where most real losses start. Keep that in mind when you read any test result: a high score is reassuring, but it is not the whole story.</p>

<h2>When a paid antivirus is actually worth it</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse2.mm.bing.net/th?q=family+using+laptop+tablet+and+phone+together&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="family using laptop tablet and phone together" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Paid suites are not stronger virus scanners. It is the same job with extras bolted on. Those extras are what you are paying for, so judge them on their own. VPNs hide your internet traffic from snoops on public Wi-Fi. Password managers store your logins so you can use a different strong password everywhere. Identity monitoring watches for your email address in leaked data. Parental controls limit what children can open and when.</p>
<p>They suit three kinds of reader. One is a household with Windows PCs, Macs, and phones, since Defender only protects Windows. One paid plan can cover them all. Parents form the second group: they want limits and reports for a child&#x27;s devices. Frequent travelers form the third: they use hotel and airport Wi-Fi often and want a VPN built into the same app. If none of those is you, the extras are features you will pay for and never open.</p>
<p>There is one more case worth naming. If you run a small business from this PC, the answer changes. Business products add central control and reporting that Defender on its own does not give you. That is a different purchase from a home suite, so do not buy a home plan and expect business features. For a home PC used for browsing, email, office work, streaming, and games, Defender plus good habits is enough.</p>
<ul><li>Devices: do you need cover for Macs or phones, not just this PC?</li><li>Children: do you need limits and reports for a child&#x27;s screen?</li><li>Travel: do you use public Wi-Fi often enough to want a VPN?</li><li>Support: do you want a phone number to call when something looks wrong?</li><li>Budget: would you rather spend that money on a backup drive?</li></ul>
<h2>Myths you can ignore about Windows 11 antivirus</h2>
<p>Free antivirus cannot be any good. That was true years ago, when the built-in option was weak and the paid products were far ahead. It is not true now. Lab scores in the last section show Defender blocking almost as much as the paid leaders, and it costs nothing. Price is not a measure of protection.</p>
<p>Running two antiviruses is better than one, people often assume. They are not. Windows allows only one real-time antivirus at a time, so installing a second one switches Defender off. Two scanners fighting over the same file can slow your PC and cause false alarms. Pick one and let it work. If you want a second opinion, use an on-demand scanner, which checks files only when you ask it to, and never runs in the background.</p>
<p>Your antivirus alone keeps you safe. That is the third myth, and the most dangerous one. The most common way people lose money now is a scam, not a virus. Think of a fake invoice, a text about a parcel, or a login page that looks real. No antivirus can stop you from typing your password into that page. Updates, backups, and a habit of pausing before you click do more than any subscription. Antivirus is one layer, and it works best with the others.</p>
<div class="note warn"><b>Warning:</b> Never download a free antivirus from a pop-up or a search advert. That is how fake antivirus, called scareware, gets onto PCs. It invents threats, then charges you to remove them. Get software only from the maker&#x27;s own site or the Microsoft Store.</div>
<div class="note"><b>Our take:</b> For most home users, Defender is all the antivirus you need. Spend the money you save on a backup drive instead.</div>
<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Is Microsoft Defender Antivirus really free?</h3>
<p>Yes. It is part of Windows 11, and there is nothing to buy, renew, or sign up for. It updates through Windows Update, so it stays current as long as your PC does.</p>
<h3>What happens if I install a paid antivirus?</h3>
<p>Defender switches its real-time protection off by itself, so the two do not clash. From then on, the paid product takes over virus scanning. If you later remove it, Defender should switch itself back on, but check Windows Security to be sure.</p>
<h3>Does Defender protect me from phishing emails?</h3>
<p>Partly. SmartScreen warns you about known phishing sites and dangerous downloads in Edge and in Windows. It cannot read your mind, so a convincing fake login page can still fool you. Pause before you enter a password, and check the address bar first.</p>
<h3>Should I run a full scan instead of a quick scan?</h3>
<p>For a routine check, a quick scan is enough. Run a full scan if a quick scan finds something, or if your PC is acting strangely for no clear reason. Full scans take much longer, so start one when you will not need the PC for a while.</p>
<h3>Do I still need antivirus if I only visit trusted sites?</h3>
<p>You still need it. Trusted sites can carry bad adverts, and a single wrong click can download malware. Defender costs you nothing and runs quietly, so there is no reason to go without it.</p>
