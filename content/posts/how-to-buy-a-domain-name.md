<div class="key"><b>The short version:</b> choose a short, easy-to-spell name, prefer .com when available, compare the <b>renewal</b> price (not just the first-year deal), make sure WHOIS privacy is included, and skip add-ons you do not need. Then point the domain at your website host.</div>

<h2>Step 1: Choose a good name</h2>
<ul>
<li><b>Short and easy to say.</b> If you have to spell it out on the phone, simplify it.</li>
<li><b>Avoid hyphens and numbers</b>; people forget them.</li>
<li><b>Check trademarks</b> so you do not build on a name a company can claim.</li>
<li><b>Check social handles</b> for the same name.</li>
<li>Look up the domain's <b>history</b> if it was used before: an old spam site can carry baggage in search engines.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Step 2: Pick an extension</h2>
<table><thead><tr><th>Extension</th><th>Use it when</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>.com</td><td>The default most people type; best for most businesses and blogs</td></tr>
<tr><td>Country codes (.uk, .de, .com.bd…)</td><td>You serve one country and want local trust</td></tr>
<tr><td>.org</td><td>Non-profits and community projects</td></tr>
<tr><td>.net, .io, .co, .app, .shop…</td><td>Your .com is taken, or the extension fits your brand</td></tr>
</tbody></table>

<h2>Step 3: Compare real prices</h2>
<p>Registrars often sell the first year cheaply and charge more on renewal. Compare:</p>
<ul>
<li>First-year price and <b>renewal price</b></li>
<li>Whether <b>WHOIS privacy</b> is free (it hides your name and address from public lookups)</li>
<li>DNS management, email forwarding and transfer fees</li>
</ul>

<h2>Step 4: Avoid unnecessary upsells</h2>
<p>At checkout you may be offered extra extensions, SEO packages, premium DNS or email plans. Most beginners need none of them. An SSL certificate is usually free with your web host (Let's Encrypt or the host's own).</p>

<h2>Step 5: Connect it to your website</h2>
<ol class="steps">
<li>Choose where the site will live: a web host, a website builder, or a free static host.</li>
<li>Either change the domain's <b>nameservers</b> to the host's, or add the <b>DNS records</b> (usually A and CNAME records) the host gives you.</li>
<li>Wait for DNS to update, from minutes to a few hours.</li>
<li>Turn on HTTPS in the host's dashboard.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Step 6: Protect it</h2>
<ul>
<li>Turn on <b>auto-renew</b> and keep your payment card current; expired domains can be snapped up quickly.</li>
<li>Enable <b>registrar lock</b> and two-factor authentication on your registrar account.</li>
<li>Use an email address you will always control as the account contact.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Can I buy a domain forever?</h3>
<p>No. Domains are registered for one to ten years at a time and must be renewed.</p>
<h3>Can I get a domain that is already taken?</h3>
<p>Sometimes: it may be for sale by its owner or on a marketplace, or it may expire. Prices for desirable names can be high.</p>
<h3>Do I need hosting to buy a domain?</h3>
<p>No. You can register a domain on its own and connect it to a website later.</p>
