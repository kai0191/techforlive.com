<div class="key"><b>The short version:</b> Bluetooth versions are backward compatible, and you only get a feature when <b>both</b> devices support it. For headphones, the codecs (AAC, aptX, LDAC, LC3) and LE Audio support matter more to sound and battery than the headline version number.</div>

<h2>What LE Audio and Auracast mean for you</h2>
<p><b>LE Audio</b> moves audio onto Bluetooth Low Energy with the new LC3 codec, which keeps quality good at lower bitrates and can improve battery life. It also enables native hearing-aid support and multi-stream audio for true wireless earbuds.</p>
<p><b>Auracast</b> is LE Audio's broadcast feature: one source (a TV, an airport gate, a gym screen) can stream to any number of nearby headphones. Like every feature, it needs support on both sides.</p>

<h2>Range and speed: the real-world picture</h2>
<p>The famous "4× range" of Bluetooth 5 applies to the long-range Coded PHY mode of Low Energy, used mostly by sensors and trackers. Headphones stream audio using other modes, so do not expect a pair of earbuds to work four times further away. Walls, bodies and Wi-Fi interference matter more than the version.</p>

<h2>Does the version matter when buying headphones?</h2>
<ul>
<li><b>Codec support</b> decides sound quality: AAC suits iPhones; aptX and LDAC are found on many Android phones.</li>
<li><b>Multipoint</b> (connecting to phone and laptop at once) is a feature the maker chooses to include; it is not guaranteed by a version.</li>
<li><b>LE Audio</b> support is worth having for future-proofing and hearing-aid features.</li>
<li>Anything from Bluetooth 5.0 up is perfectly fine for everyday listening.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Will Bluetooth 5.3 earbuds work with a 4.2 phone?</h3>
<p>Yes. Bluetooth is backward compatible; the connection simply uses the features both devices share.</p>
<h3>Can I upgrade my phone's Bluetooth version?</h3>
<p>Not really. The version depends on the radio chip; software updates occasionally add features, but cannot change the hardware.</p>
<h3>Can I add newer Bluetooth to a PC?</h3>
<p>Yes, with a USB Bluetooth adapter or by replacing a desktop's Wi-Fi/Bluetooth card.</p>
