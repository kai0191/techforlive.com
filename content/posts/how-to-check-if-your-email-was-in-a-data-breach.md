<div class="key"><b>Check now, for free:</b> enter your email at <b>haveibeenpwned.com</b>, run your password manager's breach report (Google Password Manager's Password Checkup, Apple's Security Recommendations, or your manager's "watchtower"), and turn on alerts. Then change any reused passwords, starting with email and banking.</div>

<h2>Why it matters</h2>
<p>Large companies are breached regularly, and stolen email and password lists are traded and reused. Criminals try those combinations on other sites ("credential stuffing"). If you reuse a password, one breach can unlock many accounts.</p>

<div class="note warn">Never type your password into a random "check if I was hacked" website. Legitimate tools check passwords using privacy-preserving methods, built into browsers and password managers.</div>

<h2>Your clean-up plan</h2>
<ol class="steps">
<li><b>Secure your email first.</b> Change its password to a long, unique one and turn on two-factor authentication or a passkey. Email is the key to resetting everything else.</li>
<li><b>Change passwords for the breached site</b> and every site where you reused that password.</li>
<li><b>Prioritise money and identity:</b> banking, PayPal, shopping, mobile carrier, government accounts.</li>
<li><b>Turn on passkeys or 2FA</b> wherever offered. See <a href="/what-is-a-passkey/">what a passkey is</a>.</li>
<li><b>Use a password manager</b> so every site gets a unique password you never have to remember.</li>
<li><b>Watch for follow-up scams:</b> breached data fuels convincing phishing. Our <a href="/how-to-spot-a-phishing-email/">phishing guide</a> shows the signs.</li>
<li><b>If ID numbers or card details leaked,</b> contact your bank and consider a credit freeze where available.</li>
</ol>

<h2>What the results mean</h2>
<ul>
<li><b>"Oh no, pwned!"</b> means your email appeared in at least one breach. It does not mean your account is hacked right now.</li>
<li>Check <b>what data</b> was exposed: email only, passwords (hashed or plain), phone numbers, addresses. That tells you what to change.</li>
<li>Old breaches still matter if you have not changed that password since.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Is Have I Been Pwned safe to use?</h3>
<p>Yes. It is widely used and recommended by security professionals and even government agencies; searching your email does not expose your data.</p>
<h3>My email was in 10 breaches. Should I make a new one?</h3>
<p>Not necessarily. Secure it with a strong password and 2FA, use unique passwords everywhere, and filter spam. A new address is only worth it if spam becomes unmanageable.</p>
