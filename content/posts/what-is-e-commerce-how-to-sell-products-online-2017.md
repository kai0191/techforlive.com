<div class="key"><b>E-commerce</b> is buying and selling goods or services over the internet. To start, you need three things: something people want, a place to sell it (a marketplace, your own store, or social media) and a way to get paid and deliver. Most beginners should test demand on a marketplace or social shop first, then add their own website once sales are proven.</div>

<h2>The main types of e-commerce</h2>
<table><thead><tr><th>Model</th><th>Who sells to whom</th><th>Example</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>B2C</td><td>Business to consumer</td><td>An online shoe store</td></tr>
<tr><td>B2B</td><td>Business to business</td><td>A supplier selling packaging to shops</td></tr>
<tr><td>C2C</td><td>Consumer to consumer</td><td>Selling used items on eBay or Facebook Marketplace</td></tr>
<tr><td>D2C</td><td>Brand direct to consumer, skipping retailers</td><td>A small skincare brand selling from its own site</td></tr>
</tbody></table>

<h2>Ways to get products</h2>
<ul>
<li><b>Make them</b>: crafts, art, food (check local food rules), printables.</li>
<li><b>Buy wholesale</b> and resell at a mark-up.</li>
<li><b>Print on demand</b>: upload designs; a partner prints and ships each order.</li>
<li><b>Dropshipping</b>: a supplier ships directly to your customer. Low startup cost but thin margins and less control over quality and delivery times.</li>
<li><b>Digital products</b>: templates, courses, e-books, software. No shipping and high margins.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Where to sell</h2>
<table><thead><tr><th>Option</th><th>Pros</th><th>Cons</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>Marketplaces (Amazon, eBay, Etsy, Daraz)</td><td>Built-in customers and trust</td><td>Fees per sale, strict rules, intense price competition</td></tr>
<tr><td>Your own store (Shopify, WooCommerce, a website builder)</td><td>Your brand, your customer list, lower fees per sale</td><td>You must bring the traffic yourself</td></tr>
<tr><td>Social selling (Facebook/Instagram Shops, TikTok Shop)</td><td>Reach people where they already scroll</td><td>Depends on algorithms you do not control</td></tr>
</tbody></table>

<h2>Your first sale, step by step</h2>
<ol class="steps">
<li><b>Pick a narrow niche</b> you understand, for example "eco-friendly lunch boxes for kids" rather than "kitchen products".</li>
<li><b>Check demand</b>: look at marketplace best-seller lists and search suggestions, and note what reviews complain about.</li>
<li><b>Work out your numbers</b>: product cost + shipping + platform fees + payment fees + returns. If the margin is under about 30% on physical goods, there is little room for advertising.</li>
<li><b>Choose one channel</b> and list a few products with clear photos, honest descriptions and sizes.</li>
<li><b>Set up payments and delivery</b>: marketplaces handle payments; your own store needs a payment provider and a courier.</li>
<li><b>Get the first visitors</b>: friends, local groups, short videos showing the product in use.</li>
<li><b>Learn and repeat</b>: improve listings using questions and reviews, then expand.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>How much money do I need to start?</h3>
<p>Digital products and print on demand can start with almost nothing but time. Physical stock needs money for inventory; start small.</p>
<h3>Do I need a business licence?</h3>
<p>It depends on your country and what you sell. Check your local tax office's rules for small online sellers before you scale.</p>
<h3>Is dropshipping still profitable?</h3>
<p>It can be, but competition is fierce and long shipping times hurt reviews. Branded products with reliable suppliers do better than generic items.</p>
