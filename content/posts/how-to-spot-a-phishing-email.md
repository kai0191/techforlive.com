<div class="key"><b>The 10-second check:</b> 1) Who <em>really</em> sent it (the full email address, not the name)? 2) Does it rush you or threaten you? 3) Does it want you to click, log in, pay or open a file? If the answer to 2 or 3 is yes, do not use the email: open the company's app or type its address yourself.</div>

<h2>Why phishing is harder to spot now</h2>
<p>Old scams were full of spelling mistakes. Today AI tools write fluent, personalised messages, and scammers copy real logos and layouts perfectly. That is why the checks below focus on <b>behaviour</b> (what the message wants you to do), not grammar.</p>

<h2>The warning signs</h2>
<ol class="steps">
<li><b>Mismatched sender.</b> "PayPal Support" sending from <code>paypal-secure-alerts@gmail.com</code> or a look-alike domain such as <code>paypa1.com</code>.</li>
<li><b>Urgency or fear.</b> "Your account will be closed in 24 hours", "Unusual sign-in detected", "Final notice".</li>
<li><b>Links that do not match.</b> On a computer, hover over the link; on a phone, press and hold to preview it. The real destination is what matters.</li>
<li><b>Unexpected attachments,</b> especially .zip, .html, .iso or documents that ask you to "enable content".</li>
<li><b>Requests for codes.</b> No real company asks you to read out a one-time code it just sent you.</li>
<li><b>Unusual payment methods:</b> gift cards, crypto, wire transfers to new accounts.</li>
<li><b>QR codes in emails or letters</b> ("quishing") that lead to fake login pages, bypassing email link scanners.</li>
<li><b>Too-good-to-be-true offers:</b> refunds, prizes, packages you did not order.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Text messages and calls</h2>
<p>The same tricks arrive by SMS ("smishing") and phone ("vishing"): fake parcel delivery fees, toll charges, bank fraud alerts. Banks will never ask you to move money to a "safe account". Hang up and call the number on the back of your card.</p>

<h2>If you already clicked</h2>
<ol class="steps">
<li><b>Did not enter anything?</b> Close the page. Run a security scan to be safe.</li>
<li><b>Entered a password?</b> Change it immediately on the real site, and anywhere else you reused it. Turn on two-factor authentication.</li>
<li><b>Entered card or bank details?</b> Call your bank using the official number and ask them to block the card.</li>
<li><b>Opened an attachment?</b> Disconnect from the internet, run a full antivirus scan, and change passwords from a different, clean device.</li>
<li><b>Report it</b>: use your email app's "Report phishing" button so filters learn.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Can I get hacked just by opening an email?</h3>
<p>Very rarely with an updated email app. The danger comes from clicking links, opening attachments or replying with information.</p>
<h3>The email used my real name and address. Is it real?</h3>
<p>Not necessarily. Personal details leak in data breaches and are used to make scams convincing.</p>
