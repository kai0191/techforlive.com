<h2>How to check battery health on Android</h2>
<p>Battery health means how much charge the battery can hold compared with when it was new. A phone battery uses lithium-ion cells, and charging cycles gradually reduce capacity.</p>
<p>Android does not always show this number. Apple puts a Battery Health percentage in Settings on every iPhone, but most Android phones do not. What you can see depends on the phone you own.</p>
<p>Start with the battery information your phone exposes. Then run a charging test at home. Together, these checks can show whether the battery is worn or something else is wrong.</p>

<h2>Where battery usage and health live on stock Android</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse4.mm.bing.net/th?q=Pixel+phone+on+a+desk+showing+the+battery+usage+list&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="Pixel phone on a desk showing the battery usage list" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>On a Google Pixel or another phone running close to stock Android, open Settings &gt; Battery. You will see the current charge level, an estimate of remaining time, and apps using the most power since the last full charge. An app with a large share can drain a healthy battery quickly.</p>
<p>Some phones add a diagnostics page through the Phone app. Type <em>#</em>#4636#<em>#</em>. On supported devices, a hidden menu appears with a Phone information option. Google has restricted parts of this menu on newer Android versions, and many phones show nothing.</p>
<p>If Phone information opens, look for Battery health, battery scale in mAh, or temperature. The fields vary by device, and some show nothing useful.</p>
<p>Recent Pixel phones may show manufacture date and cycle count under Battery information. A manufacture date mainly shows the battery&#x27;s age. On phones that report a cycle count, it records equivalent full charges, not each time you plug in. A high count on its own does not prove the battery needs replacement. Compare it with capacity, runtime, and shutdown symptoms.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Open Settings and tap Battery.</li><li>Tap Battery usage to see which apps drain the most power.</li><li>Look for Battery information, Battery health, or Diagnostics on the Battery screen.</li><li>If nothing appears, try <em>#</em>#4636#<em>#</em> in the Phone app and tap Phone information.</li><li>If the menu is missing or empty, move on to the charging test.</li></ol>
<div class="note"><b>Tip:</b> Battery usage sorted by app can be more useful than a health figure. A rogue app can make a healthy battery look broken.</div>
<h2>How to check battery condition on a Samsung phone or tablet</h2>
<p>Samsung gives you more battery information than many Android makers. On recent Galaxy phones and tablets, open Settings &gt; Battery, scroll down, and tap Battery information. Supported models can show estimated capacity, design capacity, and sometimes the manufacture date.</p>
<p>Older Galaxy models may show only version details. Some tablets place the page under Battery and device care, so menu wording varies between One UI versions.</p>
<p>Samsung also builds device diagnostics into its Members app. Open the app, tap Support or Get help, choose Interactive checks or Diagnostics, then select Battery status. The check reports whether the battery needs attention, based on readings the phone already takes.</p>
<p>The Members check reports whether the battery needs attention, based on readings the phone already takes. Treat it as one signal among several and compare it with the other checks.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Open Settings and tap Battery.</li><li>Scroll down and tap Battery information to see estimated capacity and, on some models, design capacity and manufacture date.</li><li>For a guided check, open Samsung Members, tap Support or Get help, then Diagnostics or Interactive checks.</li><li>Select Battery status and follow the prompts.</li><li>Note both figures if they appear.</li></ol>

<h2>What capacity figures can and cannot tell you</h2>
<p>A battery with reduced capacity can still charge to 100%. It simply stores less energy and empties sooner. That is why a full charge bar tells you nothing about wear. Only a capacity figure, or the charging test below, shows how much the battery has lost.</p>
<p>Compare estimated capacity with design capacity when your phone provides both. Design capacity is the size the battery was built with, in milliamp-hours. Estimated capacity is what the phone reckons it holds now. Manufacturers do not publish a common threshold for replacement, so the decision is symptom-driven. Check your manufacturer&#x27;s service guidance for its own advice, and weigh it against runtime and shutdown symptoms.</p>
<p>These figures are estimates. The phone calculates them from voltage and current readings, and a badly calibrated gauge can distort them. Extreme heat or cold can skew readings too.</p>
<p>A capacity figure also says nothing about whether the charging hardware works properly. The timed test covers that problem.</p>
<h2>Test whether the battery charges normally</h2>
<p>Many Android phones never show a health figure, so run a timed charging test. It answers a different question from the menus: is the battery and charging system behaving normally? You need your phone, its charger, and about half an hour.</p>
<p>Let the phone fall below 20% first. Plug in the charger you normally use and leave the phone alone for 30 minutes with the screen off. Note how much the percentage rises. There is no published figure for what the gain should be, so the test is comparative. Repeat it with a different cable and charger and compare the two gains. If the gain jumps with the second set, the first cable or charger was at fault.</p>
<p>Charging often slows near full, so start the test from a similar percentage each time you repeat it. A test that starts at 60% tells you less than one that starts at 15%.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Let the battery drain to 20% or below, then note the exact percentage and time.</li><li>Plug in the original cable and charger, or a known-good replacement. Leave the phone unused with the screen off for 30 minutes.</li><li>Note the new percentage and compare the gain with the result expected from your phone&#x27;s charging specification.</li><li>Feel the phone. If it is too hot to hold comfortably, stop the test and let it cool.</li><li>Repeat once with a different cable and plug. If the result changes, the cable or charger was likely at fault.</li></ol>
<div class="note warn"><b>Warning:</b> If the phone only charges when you wiggle the cable, the port may contain lint or be damaged. Clean it gently with a wooden toothpick, never metal.</div>

<h3>Myths you can ignore</h3>
<p>You do not need to drain the battery to 0% regularly. That habit stresses lithium-ion cells, which prefer partial charges. Leaving a phone charging overnight will not normally make it catch fire. Modern phones manage charging at full.</p>
<p>Third-party apps claiming a precise battery health score need caution. A normal app cannot read true capacity on most Android phones because the system does not expose it. These apps estimate from charge rates or display unreliable figures.</p>
<p>Heat is the symptom you should not ignore. A phone consistently hot while idle or charging may have an app, battery, or charging circuit problem. Heat also speeds up battery wear.</p>
<div class="note"><b>Our take:</b> The charging test is the most useful home check when your phone hides capacity figures. A repair shop is the next step when results stay unclear.</div>
<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Why is there no battery health option in my Android settings?</h3>
<p>Most Android manufacturers do not show a health percentage like Apple. Samsung uses its battery page and Members app, while other phones may require a service-centre check.</p>
<h3>Does <em>#</em>#4636#<em>#</em> work on every Android phone?</h3>
<p>No. It is a hidden diagnostic menu supported on some devices, but newer Android versions restrict parts of it and many phones show nothing. An empty or missing menu is normal.</p>
<h3>How long should an Android battery last before replacement?</h3>
<p>Replacement timing varies with charging habits, heat, workload, and the phone&#x27;s battery design. Replace it when capacity has dropped noticeably, runtime is poor, shutdowns begin, or your manufacturer recommends service.</p>
<h3>What if none of the battery checks gives useful data?</h3>
<p>Back up your phone first. Then take it to the manufacturer&#x27;s service centre or a reputable repair shop. Technicians can measure charging current and battery condition with proper tools. A missing menu or an unclear test is normal on many phones, and a shop test settles it.</p>
<h3>Are battery health apps from the Play Store accurate?</h3>
<p>Treat their results as estimates, not measurements. They can help track charging behaviour over time, but they cannot reliably measure true capacity on phones that hide it. Built-in tools and a repair-shop test are better.</p>
<h3>Can a bad charger damage my battery?</h3>
<p>A poor-quality or mismatched charger usually charges slowly because the phone limits its rate. Counterfeit adapters without proper safety certification carry greater risk, so use the manufacturer&#x27;s charger or a reputable certified one.</p>
