<div class="key"><b>Honest answer:</b> at home on your own secured Wi-Fi, most people do not <em>need</em> a VPN for basic safety, because websites already use HTTPS encryption. A VPN becomes genuinely useful on <b>public Wi-Fi</b>, when travelling, to stop your internet provider seeing which sites you visit, and for accessing your home streaming services abroad.</div>

<h2>What a VPN does</h2>
<p>A VPN (virtual private network) creates an encrypted tunnel from your device to a server run by the VPN company. Websites see the VPN server's IP address instead of yours, and your internet provider or the café Wi-Fi owner sees only encrypted traffic going to that server.</p>

<h2>What a VPN does not do</h2>
<ul>
<li>It does <b>not</b> make you anonymous: logged-in accounts, cookies and browser fingerprints still identify you.</li>
<li>It does <b>not</b> stop phishing, malware or scam calls. You still need good habits and security software.</li>
<li>It moves trust from your internet provider to the VPN company, so the provider's honesty matters.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How to choose a trustworthy VPN</h2>
<ul>
<li><b>Independent no-logs audits</b> by a known firm, published recently.</li>
<li><b>Modern protocols</b> such as WireGuard (or the provider's version of it).</li>
<li><b>A kill switch</b> that blocks traffic if the VPN drops.</li>
<li>Apps for all your devices, and clear, simple pricing.</li>
<li>Avoid unknown free VPNs: running servers costs money, and some free apps make it back by collecting and selling data.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Free things to do first</h2>
<ol class="steps">
<li>Change your router's default admin password and use WPA2 or WPA3 Wi-Fi security.</li>
<li>Update the router's firmware.</li>
<li>Turn on HTTPS-only or "always use secure connections" in your browser. See our <a href="/google-chrome-will-protect-you-from-hackers/">Chrome security checklist</a>.</li>
<li>Use a password manager and two-factor authentication.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Does a VPN slow down my internet?</h3>
<p>Slightly, because traffic travels further and is encrypted. Good providers with nearby servers have a small impact on typical home connections.</p>
<h3>Is using a VPN legal?</h3>
<p>In most countries, yes. Some countries restrict or ban VPNs; check local law when travelling.</p>
<h3>Should I leave the VPN on all the time?</h3>
<p>On public networks, yes. At home it is a personal choice: on if you want privacy from your ISP, off if you prefer maximum speed.</p>
