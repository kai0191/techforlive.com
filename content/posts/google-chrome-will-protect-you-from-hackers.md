<div class="key"><b>Do these three now:</b> keep Chrome updated (menu › Help › About Google Chrome), switch Safe Browsing to <b>Enhanced protection</b>, and run <b>Safety Check</b> to find compromised passwords and risky extensions. All three are under Settings › Privacy and security.</div>

<h2>Why the browser matters so much</h2>
<p>Your browser is where you log in, pay and download, so it is where most attacks happen: phishing pages, malicious downloads, fake extensions and stolen session cookies. Chrome has strong defences, but several are not at their strongest by default.</p>

<h2>The 10 settings</h2>
<ol class="steps">
<li><b>Update automatically and restart.</b> Chrome fixes actively exploited security holes several times a year. When the menu button shows "Update", restart promptly.</li>
<li><b>Enhanced Safe Browsing.</b> Settings › Privacy and security › Security › Enhanced protection. Chrome checks sites and downloads against Google's real-time threat data and warns about dangerous extensions. It shares more browsing data with Google, which is the trade-off.</li>
<li><b>Run Safety Check.</b> It flags passwords found in data breaches, outdated Chrome, harmful extensions and sites with unusual permissions.</li>
<li><b>Always use secure connections.</b> Turn on "Always use secure connections" so Chrome tries HTTPS first and warns before loading plain HTTP pages.</li>
<li><b>Review site permissions.</b> Settings › Privacy and security › Site settings. Remove notification permission from sites you do not recognise; spammy notifications are a common scam route.</li>
<li><b>Prune extensions.</b> Go to <code>chrome://extensions</code> and remove anything you do not use. Extensions can read pages you visit, and popular ones are sometimes sold to new owners who add malicious code.</li>
<li><b>Use passkeys or strong unique passwords.</b> Chrome's Password Manager can create and store unique passwords and passkeys and sync them across devices.</li>
<li><b>Turn on 2-Step Verification</b> for your Google account. If your account is taken over, so is everything synced to Chrome.</li>
<li><b>Be careful with downloads.</b> Only download software from the official site. Chrome warns about suspicious files; do not click past the warning.</li>
<li><b>Use separate profiles</b> for work, personal and shared family browsing, so one compromised session does not expose everything.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Is Enhanced Safe Browsing worth it?</h3>
<p>For most people, yes: it catches threats faster than standard protection. If you prefer to share less data with Google, standard protection is still valuable.</p>
<h3>Does Incognito mode protect me from hackers?</h3>
<p>No. Incognito only stops Chrome saving history and cookies on your device after you close the window. It does not hide you from websites, your network or malware.</p>
<h3>How do I know if an extension is safe?</h3>
<p>Install as few as possible, prefer well-known developers, check the permissions it asks for, and remove any that change ownership or start showing ads.</p>
