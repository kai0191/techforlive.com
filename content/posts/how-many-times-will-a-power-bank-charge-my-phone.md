<div class="key"><b>Rule of thumb:</b> expect roughly <b>60–70%</b> of a power bank's rated mAh to reach your phone. A 10,000 mAh bank typically gives about 1.5 full charges to a 4,500–5,000 mAh phone, and a 20,000 mAh bank about 3. Use the calculator below for your exact phone.</div>

<h2>Why the label overstates it</h2>
<p>Power bank capacity is quoted at the internal cell voltage, usually 3.6–3.85 V. Your phone's battery is rated at a similar voltage, so it looks like a straight comparison. But energy has to travel a longer route:</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>The bank <b>boosts</b> its cell voltage up to 5 V, 9 V or more for USB.</li>
<li>The cable loses a little as heat.</li>
<li>The phone <b>steps it back down</b> to charge its battery, and also powers itself while charging.</li>
</ol>
<p>Each step loses energy. Overall efficiency of 60–80% is normal; fast charging and warm conditions sit at the lower end.</p>

<h2>The calculation in watt-hours</h2>
<p>Convert both batteries to watt-hours (Wh) so voltages do not confuse things: <code>Wh = mAh × V ÷ 1000</code>.</p>
<table><thead><tr><th></th><th>Power bank</th><th>Phone</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>Rated</td><td>10,000 mAh × 3.7 V = 37 Wh</td><td>5,000 mAh × 3.85 V ≈ 19.3 Wh</td></tr>
<tr><td>After ~70% efficiency</td><td>≈ 26 Wh delivered</td><td>—</td></tr>
<tr><td>Full charges</td><td colspan="2">26 ÷ 19.3 ≈ <b>1.3–1.4 charges</b></td></tr>
</tbody></table>

<h2>Flying with a power bank</h2>
<p>Airlines generally require power banks in <b>carry-on</b> luggage, not checked bags. Under widely used IATA guidance, banks up to 100 Wh are allowed without approval (that covers most banks up to about 27,000 mAh at 3.7 V); 100–160 Wh usually needs airline approval. Some airlines have added extra rules, such as banning in-flight use, so check yours before travelling.</p>

<h2>Get more out of every charge</h2>
<ul>
<li>Use a short, good-quality USB-C cable.</li>
<li>Charge the phone while it is idle or switched to airplane mode.</li>
<li>Keep the bank cool; heat lowers efficiency and ages its cells.</li>
<li>Recharge the bank every few months if you store it, ideally around half full.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Does a higher-watt power bank charge more times?</h3>
<p>No. Watts decide <em>speed</em>; watt-hours (or mAh) decide how much energy it holds.</p>
<h3>Why is my power bank empty after one charge?</h3>
<p>It may be smaller in real capacity than claimed (common with unbranded banks), worn out, or losing a lot to heat during fast charging.</p>
