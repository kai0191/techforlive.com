<div class="key"><b>The realistic path:</b> pick one engine, finish tiny games, join game jams, and build a portfolio of playable projects. Studios hire based on what you have made far more than on certificates. You can start today with free tools.</div>

<h2>"Game developer" is many jobs</h2>
<table><thead><tr><th>Role</th><th>What they do</th><th>Key skills</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>Gameplay programmer</td><td>Makes mechanics, controls and AI work</td><td>C#, C++, maths, debugging</td></tr>
<tr><td>Game designer</td><td>Designs rules, levels, systems and balance</td><td>Prototyping, writing, playtesting</td></tr>
<tr><td>Technical artist</td><td>Connects art and code: shaders, effects, tools</td><td>Shaders, scripting, art pipelines</td></tr>
<tr><td>3D/2D artist, animator</td><td>Creates characters, environments and motion</td><td>Blender, Maya, Photoshop, drawing</td></tr>
<tr><td>QA tester</td><td>Finds and reports bugs systematically</td><td>Attention to detail, clear writing</td></tr>
<tr><td>Producer</td><td>Plans schedules and keeps teams shipping</td><td>Organisation, communication</td></tr>
</tbody></table>

<h2>Choose your first engine</h2>
<table><thead><tr><th>Engine</th><th>Language</th><th>Best for</th><th>Cost</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>Godot</td><td>GDScript (Python-like), C#</td><td>2D games, beginners, small teams</td><td>Free and open source</td></tr>
<tr><td>Unity</td><td>C#</td><td>Mobile, indie, 2D and 3D</td><td>Free personal tier; check current licence terms</td></tr>
<tr><td>Unreal Engine</td><td>C++ and Blueprints (visual scripting)</td><td>High-end 3D, large studios</td><td>Free to start; royalties above a revenue threshold</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>There is no wrong choice. Godot is the gentlest start; Unity and Unreal appear most often in job listings.</p>

<h2>A 12-month plan</h2>
<ol class="steps">
<li><b>Months 1–2:</b> learn the basics of programming through the engine's official beginner tutorials. Recreate Pong and Flappy Bird.</li>
<li><b>Months 3–4:</b> make a small original game in four weeks: one mechanic, three levels, a start and end screen. Finish it.</li>
<li><b>Months 5–6:</b> join a game jam (itch.io lists many, and the Global Game Jam runs yearly). Shipping a game in 48 hours teaches scope like nothing else.</li>
<li><b>Months 7–9:</b> go deeper in your chosen role: maths for programmers, level design for designers, Blender for artists.</li>
<li><b>Months 10–12:</b> polish your best two projects, publish them free on itch.io, and write short breakdowns of how you solved problems.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Building a portfolio that gets interviews</h2>
<ul>
<li>Two or three <b>finished, playable</b> games beat ten abandoned prototypes.</li>
<li>Show your part clearly if it was a team project.</li>
<li>Include short videos or GIFs; recruiters may not download builds.</li>
<li>Put code on GitHub with a readable README.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Do I need a degree?</h3>
<p>Not necessarily. A computer science or game development degree helps, especially for programming roles and visas, but portfolios decide most hires.</p>
<h3>Which programming language should I learn?</h3>
<p>C# if you choose Unity or Godot (C#), C++ for Unreal and engine work, GDScript for Godot beginners.</p>
<h3>Can I make money from indie games?</h3>
<p>Some developers do, but most indie games earn little. Treat your first games as learning and portfolio pieces.</p>
